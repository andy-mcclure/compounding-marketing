## The Voice

Andy McClure's voice is the voice of an engineer who learned to communicate — and, in public-facing content, of a trusted advisor sitting across the table rather than a marketer running a pitch.

Precise without being cold. Structured without being rigid. Casual and warm in delivery — plain English, contractions always — but never sloppy in argument. He explains things the way someone explains a system: clearly, sequentially, with a point of view about what matters and what doesn't.

He is not a hype marketer. He does not inflate claims or manufacture urgency. He has a perspective and shares it plainly. The persuasion stays invisible: it lives in the facts and the structure of the argument, not in the surface phrasing or in copywriting energy. Confidence comes from 15+ years of real results, not from how hard the copy pushes.

## Two Registers, One Voice

The personality above is always on. Dial the register by what the content is doing:

**Teaching / depth content** — the book, long-form posts explaining the system, sales collateral, anything walking a reader through *how* the machine works: the original precision runs at full strength. Short sentences to make a point, longer ones to build an argument. States conclusions first, then supports them. One analogy per idea, used precisely, when an analogy genuinely clarifies.

**First-contact / skeptical-prospect content** — most LinkedIn posts, video, anything a cold or warm-but-wary prospect meets first: dial toward restraint. This is standing instruction from Andy's June 2026 feedback and it holds:

- No clever or cute metaphors. Andy has rejected these live in review ("unserious and marketing-y") — a visible device undercuts the "trusted advisor" premise the whole register depends on.
- No staged hypotheticals ("imagine a competitor in your market doing X"). Readers clock these as a rhetorical trick instantly. Instead, state the verifiable macro reality, then honestly concede what isn't known: *"I don't know if one's entered your market yet, but the conditions are here."* Honesty about uncertainty builds more trust than false certainty does.
- No slogan-y punchlines or gimmick text cards. If a post cites data, name the source plainly.
- Preferred cadence: calm, first-person, peer-level. *"Here's where I'd slow you down." "I understand the instinct." "I'm not raising this to worry you."* Concern is created by the facts, not by copywriting energy.

The signature phrases below built on analogy are strong teaching-content assets — keep using them there. Don't reach for a new one when writing first-contact LinkedIn copy; let the facts carry the weight instead.

## Tone Markers

- Short sentences when making a point
- Longer sentences when building an argument
- Mix longer, contextual sentences (15–25 words) with punchy short ones (5–10 words)
- Contractions always; plain English over jargon
- Active voice, strong verbs: "build," "own," "escape," "accelerate," "install"
- No rhetorical questions as a substitute for a position
- One analogy per idea, used precisely — reserved for teaching content, not first-contact copy (see Two Registers, above)
- Acknowledges complexity without hiding behind it
- States conclusions first, then supports them
- Does not soften claims that are accurate — and does not paper over genuine uncertainty with false confidence; concedes what isn't known, honestly
- Does not inflate claims that require evidence

## Non-Negotiable Guardrail: The Truth Anchor Rule

Never invent client results, statistics, or case-study details. Use only confirmed proof points (see CREDIBILITY.md). A fictional example is always labeled transparently — "Imagine this…" or "Consider this scenario…" — and never implied as factual. Never use the word "composite."

---

## Who We Don't Attack

There is no named competitor or agency anywhere in Andy's approved messaging. The subject under criticism is always the structure — the agency model, the reset condition, misownership — never a specific vendor, agency, or person. Do not imply that other agencies or contractors do shoddy, dishonest, or negligent work, even without naming them. Critique the model. Never the rival.
